Most robot learning papers are tested in simulation on rigs above 20K. Basically, 16 out of 20 papers, only two were under 5K for rigs. But does this work on low cost hardware? And that's actually what small teams want to know. So, what we did is that we tested 37 claims from 20 robot learning papers. Our rig was basically an SO 101 ARM and a Jetson Orin NX 16 gigabyte of memory for a total of nearly 1K and a half and just $50 of compute. So basically, we picked papers from Stack VRun, world models, VLAs, failure monitors, chunking edge, latencies. We use two rules for every test: bar committed in Git before testing, and then the cheapest baseline run first. Same rows, copy last, commanded joins, episode length. And the result was very surprising. Only 12 out of 13 validate, 18 fails. And this is a surprise because several methods lows basically to that dumb baseline, not just to the bar. And we distinct three ways these papers fail. The first is too slow. Adaptive chunking takes, for example, nearly 5 seconds on the Orin, which has a budget of 0.3 seconds. Then you have the judge that lows to episode length. So the robot model basically has 0.80 rock, but the length alone is 0.9. And then the world model latent lows to the commanded joins and action only baseline. So the advice always starts a restart from the cheapest baseline. And then of course we checked everything on our Git history, no bar, written after result. Everything is public, every number can be reproduced from the repo.
