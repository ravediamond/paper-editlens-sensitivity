Re-testing 22 robot-learning papers on a €1,350 arm, with an audit of our own pre-registration

We took 37 claims from 22 robot-learning papers and checked them on an SO-101 arm driven by a Jetson Orin NX, about €1,350 of hardware and $45.10 of compute. Twelve held. Before each test we ran the cheapest baseline first and committed the pass mark to git, so the bar was fixed before we saw the outcome. The failures cluster into three groups, which we propose as a rough taxonomy: methods too slow for real-time control on an edge GPU, zero-shot judges beaten by a trivial signal, and latent planners that do worse than simply using the commanded joints. Then we turned the same check on ourselves. Our commit history shows most gates were set before the result came in; for a few, it can't tell us which came first.
