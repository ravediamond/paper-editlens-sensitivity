Robot-learning papers are more and more experimented in simulation or on rigs that cost more than $20k. But small teams often has to decide whether a method will work on far cheaper hardware. In this paper, we re-test 37 claims from
22 robot-learning papers on a €1350 rig, SO-101 arm with a Nvidia Jetson Orin NX and find that only 12 of them
hold. For each claim, we commit the bar before the test and run the cheapest baseline first, on the same rows. We find
that in multiple failures, this baseline is what the method loses against. We propose three classes of failure: methods too slow
for edge GPU, zero-shot judges that lose to the episode length, and latent planners that lose to the commanded joints.
Our results suggest that a re-test on a cheap rig should always start from its cheapest baseline. Every number in the paper can be
replicated from the public repository.
